Why light sleepers are quietly switching to a warm sleep mask

It blocks every bit of light and adds a gentle, steady warmth around the eyes. Here’s what changed for people who tried it for a month.

By Maya Lind, sleep and wellness writer · Oct 20, 2026 · 5 min read

If you wake up at the first hint of daylight, or lie awake replaying the day, you already know the usual advice. Darker curtains. No screens after nine. A cooler room. It helps, but for a lot of light sleepers it isn’t enough.

Somna started with a simple question from its founder, a product designer who spent years waking at 5 a.m. to street lights and early sunrises: what if a sleep mask did more than block light?

Total darkness, plus a little warmth

Most masks leak light at the nose and press on the eyes. Somna is shaped with deep eye cups, so nothing touches your lashes, and a contoured nose bridge that seals out light even when you sleep on your side.

Inside is a thin, flexible heat layer. Press the button and it warms to one of three levels, from barely-there to cosy, then switches itself off after 20 minutes.

How people use it

  1. Wind down. Put the mask on in bed and choose a warmth level.
  2. Let it switch off. After 20 minutes the heat turns off on its own. The mask keeps blocking light.
