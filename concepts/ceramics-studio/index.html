Hollow & Hand

Wheel-thrown tableware, made in small batches in Portland, Oregon.

Cobalt, celadon, ashThree glazes, mixed in the studio.
Fired twiceBisque at 1,000°C, glaze at 1,240°C.

Every piece starts as a handful of clay.

We throw, trim and glaze each one by hand, so no two mugs are the same. That is the point.

The everyday set

Made in batches of twenty or fewer. When a glaze sells out, it waits for the next firing.

Slow on purpose

A mug takes about three weeks from wedge to shelf. Most of that time is waiting: for clay to dry, for the kiln to cool.

Day 1Throw

Clay is wedged to remove air, centered on the wheel and pulled up into shape. Walls are thin, so they have to be even.

Day 6Trim

Once leather-hard, each piece goes back on the wheel. We trim the foot, add the maker's mark and take away what it doesn't need.

Day 21Fire

Bisque firing, glazing, then a second firing that takes 14 hours. We open the kiln the next morning, and sometimes we get a surprise.

Make your own

Small groups, six people at most. Clay, tools, glaze and two firings are included.
